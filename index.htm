AES-HAA

AES-HAA is a low-cost, COTS-based hardware survivability platform engineered to enhance the reliability and lifespan of electronic and battery systems operating in the extreme subzero temperatures (-35°C) and low-pressure conditions of Ladakh. It integrates a zero-parasitic 3D spatial-thermal BMS that recycles internal Joule heat to warm frozen battery cells, an adaptive dynamic DRAM refresh system with gate-level radiation hardening (TMR) to prevent cosmic-ray bit-flips, and a dual-mode fluid loop with anti-arcing coatings. By avoiding expensive military-grade components, it delivers an affordable, ruggedized plug-and-play solution that prevents mission dropouts and electronic burnout across defense UAVs, radar outposts, and high-altitude telecom infrastructure.

Problem & Proposed Solution

The Problem We Are Addressing

Operating electronics and batteries at altitudes above 10,000–18,000 ft in Ladakh faces three severe environmental threats:

  • Extreme Subzero Temperatures (down to -35°C): Battery capacity collapses due to frozen electrolytes, causing premature undervoltage shutdowns in drones and communication radios. Standard heating pads drain the battery they are supposed to protect.
  • Low Atmospheric Pressure (Thin Air): Natural air cooling fails, causing power amplifiers and processors to overheat. The dielectric strength of air drops significantly, leading to electrical arcing and component burnout across PCB traces.
  • Elevated Cosmic Radiation: High-altitude cosmic rays trigger Single Event Upsets (SEUs)—flipping memory bits in DRAM and causing flight controllers, avionics, and GPS systems to crash mid-mission.
  • The Cost/Supply Hurdle: True military-grade (Mil-Spec) radiation/thermal-hardened hardware is extremely expensive and restricted by foreign export bans (ITAR).

Our Proposed Hardware Solution

The Adaptive Electronics & Survivability Platform (AES-HAA) solves this using a 3-pillar, low-cost COTS (Commercial Off-The-Shelf) architecture:

  • Pillar 1: 3D Spatial-Thermal BMS (Power & Battery) Zero-Parasitic Joule Self-Heating: Instead of power-hungry external heaters, it traps the battery’s own internal resistance ( I 2 R I 2 R ) heat to warm up frozen cells from the inside out. Dynamic Current Steering: Uses discrete N-MOSFETs to route charging/discharging current away from hot inner cells toward freezing outer cells when Δ T > 3 ∘ C ΔT>3 ∘ C , cutting cell state-of-charge divergence ( Δ SoC ΔSoC ) from 68% down to 15%.
  • Pillar 2: Rad-Hard & Adaptive Computing (Avionics & Logic) Adaptive Dynamic Refresh (ATREF): At -35°C, silicon leakage drops dramatically. The system dynamically slows down DRAM refresh cycles, slashing standby power by up to 70%. Gate-Level Radiation Immunity (TMR): Uses low-cost discrete logic (74HC00 + 74HC153 multiplexer) to implement hardware-level Triple Modular Redundancy (TMR). If a cosmic ray flips a bit, the majority voting circuit instantly corrects it in hardware with zero latency.
  • Pillar 3: Dual-Mode Loop & Rugged Shell (Structure & Cooling) Low-Pressure Phase-Change Loop: A sealed binary ethanol-water heat-pipe loop provides passive thermal clamping—enabling a sub-98-second cold-start jumpstart while preventing overheating in low-density air. Anti-Arcing & Insulation Wrap: Encased in a lightweight Silica Aerogel blanket for thermal retention, combined with a Parylene-C conformal coating that completely prevents dielectric breakdown and arcing in thin air.

Feasibility, Viability & Benefits

Feasibility & Risk Mitigation

Technical Feasibility:

The system is exceptionally easy to manufacture and assemble using 100% low-cost COTS components (~₹2,800 BOM) on standard PCBs, requiring only simple analog resistor-divider tuning for deterministic calibration without complex software.

Potential Challenges & Risks:

Key risks include battery SoC imbalance when temperatures swing from -30°C to +25°C, memory data corruption if dynamic refresh rates are over-slowed, and thin-air electrical arcing at high altitudes.

Overcoming Strategy:

These are countered by a passive binary fluid jacket that thermally clamps cells (10°C–20°C), hardware-clamped refresh limits ( T m a x ≤ 64 ms T max ​ ≤64 ms ) with gate-level TMR parity logic to stop bit loss, and a Parylene-C anti-arcing coating.

Impact & Expected Benefits

Target Audience / Beneficiaries:

Primary users are the Armed Forces and DRDO operating UAVs, avionics, and radar systems at high-altitude forward bases, alongside civilian infrastructure operators managing remote Himalayan 4G/5G telecom towers, weather stations, and avalanche warning radar networks.

Economic & Social Impact:

Drastically cuts deployment costs by replacing expensive, ITAR-restricted military-grade ICs with standard COTS silicon (~₹2,800 BOM), while socially enabling uninterrupted 24/7 communications, automated disaster alerts, and secure border operations in isolated regions

Environmental Impact:

Operates with 0W parasitic power waste by recycling internal I 2 R I 2 R battery heat, which preserves energy and significantly reduces hazardous battery and electronic waste in the ecologically fragile Himalayan ecosystem.
