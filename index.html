AES-HAA

Adaptive Electronics & Survivability Platform for High-Altitude Operations

A low-cost COTS hardware platform that keeps batteries and electronics alive at −35°C and high altitudes in Ladakh — without expensive military-grade components.

Problem & Proposed Solution

The Problem

Electronics and batteries operating above 10,000–18,000 ft in Ladakh face three critical threats:

  • Extreme cold (−35°C) — Battery capacity collapses due to frozen electrolytes. External heaters drain the very battery they try to protect.
  • Low atmospheric pressure — Air cooling fails and dielectric strength drops, causing arcing and component burnout.
  • Cosmic radiation — High-altitude SEUs flip bits in DRAM, crashing flight controllers and GPS mid-mission.
  • Cost barrier — True Mil-Spec radiation/thermal-hardened parts are expensive and restricted by ITAR.

Our Solution — AES-HAA

A 3-pillar, low-cost COTS architecture that delivers military-grade survivability:

  • 3D Spatial-Thermal BMS — Zero-parasitic Joule self-heating traps the battery’s own I²R heat. Dynamic current steering via discrete N-MOSFETs cuts ΔSoC from 68% → 15%.
  • Rad-Hard Adaptive Computing — Adaptive DRAM refresh (up to 70% lower standby power at −35°C) + gate-level TMR using discrete logic for zero-latency SEU correction.
  • Dual-Mode Thermal Loop — Sealed ethanol-water heat-pipe + Silica Aerogel + Parylene-C coating for cold-start jumpstart and anti-arcing protection in thin air.

Slides 4 & 5

Feasibility, Viability & Benefits

Feasibility & Risk Mitigation

Technical Feasibility

Built entirely with low-cost COTS parts (≈ ₹2,800 BOM) on standard PCBs. Calibration uses simple analog resistor dividers — no complex firmware dependency.

Key Risks

SoC imbalance during large temperature swings, potential data loss if refresh is slowed too aggressively, and thin-air arcing at altitude.

Mitigation Strategy

Passive binary fluid jacket clamps cell temperature, hardware-clamped refresh limits (Tmax ≤ 64 ms) + TMR voting, and Parylene-C anti-arcing coating.

Impact & Expected Benefits

Target Users

Armed Forces & DRDO (UAVs, avionics, radar) and civilian operators of high-altitude telecom towers, weather stations, and avalanche warning systems.

Economic & Social Impact

Replaces expensive ITAR-restricted parts with affordable COTS silicon. Enables reliable 24/7 communications and disaster-alert systems in remote Himalayan regions.

Environmental Impact

Zero parasitic heater power by recycling internal I²R heat. Reduces battery and electronics waste in the fragile high-altitude ecosystem.
